The original Avalon

Before the Legacy Edition there was Avalon: a freeware DOS RPG, released in 1998 and written from scratch in Turbo Pascal. It is still free, and still runs great in DOSBox.

A classic 2D RPG from 1998

The story is about Mace, a hero who has to save his fellow villagers. The last of humanity have stranded on a distant planet after the destruction of Earth. They built a small settlement and did not expect any other hostile life on the planet. But as it soon turned out, appearances were deceptive.

It will take all of Mace's courage and skill to face what's coming.

The story behind Avalon

Let us take you back in time for a moment. Back to the '90s. A time of dial-up internet, 8-bit and 16-bit computers, chunky CRT monitors, and the two of us — brothers and MSX enthusiasts — working on the RPG Avalon…

Between 1996 and 1998, we — brothers Jeroen (b. 1979) and Bowie Derwort (b. 1982) — worked on the original DOS version of Avalon. We both still lived with our parents; Jeroen had the attic room, Bowie the floor below. Especially on weekends, we'd constantly run up and down the stairs to show each other the latest additions. “Check out what I just programmed!” or “Look at my latest monster!”

Let's be honest: it wasn't always fun and games. There were stretches where motivation was nowhere to be found. But thanks to a good dose of discipline (and probably because we kept pushing each other) we always picked it back up. And it paid off: in 2000, Avalon won the coveted ZDNet Award for Best Shareware Strategy Game, and has been downloaded over 200,000 times!

Pixel art before it was cool

Avalon was definitely not our first project together, but it was our very first game for PC/DOS. We drew our inspiration from legendary MSX games like SD Snatcher (one of the lesser-known masterpieces by famed Konami game designer Hideo Kojima), Pumpkin Adventure, Ys III, and Dragon Slayer VI. Even though PCs could already handle higher resolutions back then, we deliberately took a step back. We chose a lower resolution with thick, square pixels to evoke that wonderful nostalgic feeling. In a way, we were making “pixel art” on purpose, at a time when that was still just the norm!

Soundtrack

The music in Avalon was composed entirely by us. And the unique part: we used MiG Tracker for it, a DOS music program Jeroen had built himself. It's based on ‘Moonblaster’, a program for MSX computers. In the Legacy Edition, alongside the MP3 versions, you can still listen to the original MiG Tracker versions. Although for this new release we did run the audio through the car wash. Now it sounds just as epic as it did in our heads back then. Listen to the soundtrack.

Teenagers, family, and nuclear weapons

Avalon is full of characters whose names were inspired by our friends and family. Ever run into the alien Esor Amreh? Reverse that name and you get Rose Herma — our parents' first names!

For the Legacy Edition we improved the game design in a number of places. We tried to preserve that typical '90s feel while bringing it a little more up to date at the same time. We also had some second thoughts about a few of our old choices. Things that seem brilliant to you as a 16-year-old look a little different years later. Take the nuclear bomb we put in the game as an item. We thought it was hilarious back then. What happens when you use it? Everything dies. Including you, the player. Ah well — we left it in. It's part of the charm.

The magic

Over the years, we'd occasionally get feedback from people who had thoroughly enjoyed playing Avalon. Reviews like that mean a lot to us, and they've been an important source of motivation for us both.

So we hope that when you boot up Avalon Legacy Edition, you'll feel that pure, typical '90s game feeling again, even if just for a moment. The simplicity, the atmosphere and the magic of back then are something really special. That's exactly what we want to pass on to you with this Legacy Edition.

Have fun playing!
Jeroen & Bowie Derwort

Download Avalon

Avalon is freeware, so the download is completely free. It runs best in DOSBox.

The easy way: D-Fend Reloaded

First download and install D-Fend Reloaded, a front-end for DOSBox. Then download the Avalon game package and import it into D-Fend, and you will automatically run the game with the best settings.

1 · Get D-Fend Reloaded 2 · Avalon D-Fend package (ZIP)

The manual way: plain DOSBox

Alternatively, download DOSBox and Avalon separately, and run the game by mounting its directory from within DOSBox. In the DOSBox config, under [sblaster], set irq=5.

ZDNet Shareware Award, 2000

Avalon received the ZDNet Shareware Award in 2000. Out of all the freeware and shareware games released that year, the jury picked Avalon as the best in the category Strategy.

The RPG Maker remake

A developer called Huepow00 worked on a remake of the original Avalon in RPG Maker 2000. Unfortunately the project stopped before it was completed. Huepow00 also made several remixes of the soundtrack, which you can hear on the music page.

Avalon: Legacy Edition

More than 25 years later, the original creators rebuilt Avalon themselves: the same world, story and quests, redrawn and refined for modern systems, and with the 1998 original hidden inside.
